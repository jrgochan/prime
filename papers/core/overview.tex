\begin{abstract}
We describe a Lean~4 formalization---the \emph{Cathedral}---that reduces
the Riemann Hypothesis to the decay of the Nyman--Beurling distance
$d_N^2 \to 0$, proved equivalent to RH via the biconditional theorem
\texttt{nyman\_beurling\_equivalence}.

The formalization comprises 161 active Lean files
(with 127 archived) across 26 modules totaling 39{,}375 lines.
The crown theorem depends
on exactly \textbf{two mathematical axioms}: one encoding the
Hardy--Littlewood mean-value bound for $1/\zeta(s)$ on the critical line,
and one encoding the Hadamard zero-counting zeta lower bound.
The converse direction ($d_N^2 \to 0 \implies \mathrm{RH}$) uses
\textbf{zero custom axioms}---it is pure Lean/Mathlib.

The forward direction ($\mathrm{RH} \implies d_N^2 \to 0$) is assembled
via the \emph{Mellin Crown}: the Mellin/Plancherel isometry maps the
$L^2(0,1)$ residual to the critical line, where the Mellin variance is
bounded by $C/\log N$ under RH. The Parseval bridge connecting the two
domains is \emph{fully proved} (zero axioms, zero sorry).
Companion Rust experiments validate the Parseval bridge to
$6.6 \times 10^{-6}$ relative error at $N = 2000$.
\end{abstract}

\section{What Did We Do?}

We used \textbf{Lean~4} to reduce the 167-year-old Riemann Hypothesis
to two precisely stated, well-understood analytic bounds. Everything
else---the Nyman--Beurling theory,
Rank-1 Mellin separation, the Parseval bridge, the Mellin/Plancherel
isometry, and the calculus limit $C/\log N \to 0$---is compiler-verified.

\textbf{We did not prove RH.} We built a machine-verified containment
vessel that identifies exactly which mathematical facts remain unformalized,
proves everything else, and reduces the entire century-old problem to
a clean modular architecture with two axiom sockets on the crown
theorem's critical path (${\sim}55$ axioms total in the active codebase;
${\sim}53$ are on alternative paths and do not affect the crown theorem).

\section{The Crown Theorem}

\begin{verbatim}
theorem nyman_beurling_equivalence :
    RiemannHypothesis <->
    (forall eps > 0, exists N0, forall N >= N0,
       exists v, integral |1 - bdLinComb N v|^2 < eps)
\end{verbatim}

\noindent RH holds if and only if the B\'aez-Duarte distance
$d_N^2 \to 0$. The proof decomposes into two pillars:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pillar~I (Converse)}: $d_N^2 \to 0 \implies \mathrm{RH}$.
  Via the Rank-1 Mellin Miracle: $M[h_k](\rho) = 1/(k(\rho-1))$ at
  every $\zeta$ zero, giving a rank-1 tensor factorization.
  Cauchy--Schwarz yields $d_N^2 \ge (2\sigma - 1)t^2/(|\rho|^4|\rho-1|^2)> 0$
  whenever $\sigma \ne 1/2$.
  \textbf{Zero custom axioms.} Zero sorry. Pure Lean/Mathlib.

\item \textbf{Pillar~II (Forward)}: $\mathrm{RH} \implies d_N^2 \to 0$.
  Via the \emph{Mellin Crown}:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item RH $\to$ Mellin variance $\le C/\log N$ [\texttt{critical\_line\_mellin\_variance}, Axiom~1]
    \item Parseval bridge: $\int_0^1 (1-f_N)^2\,dx = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int |M_{r_N}(1/2+it)|^2\,dt$ [\texttt{parseval\_bridge\_white}, \textbf{PROVED}]
    \item $C/\log N < \varepsilon$ for $N$ large [\texttt{log\_grows\_unboundedly}, \textbf{PROVED}]
  \end{enumerate}
  \textbf{Two axioms. Zero sorry.}
\end{itemize}

\section{The Mellin Crown}

The key innovation of v11: instead of bounding the $L^2$ residual via
real-variable methods (Perron contour $\to$ Mertens bound $\to$ Abel summation
$\to$ Gram form), we stay in the frequency domain throughout:

\[
  \int_0^1 |1 - f_N(x)|^2\,dx
  = \frac{1}{2\pi} \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |M_{r_N}(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^2\,dt
  \le \frac{C}{\log N}
\]

The left equality is the Parseval bridge (\textbf{fully proved}). The right
inequality is the crown axiom. Under RH, the Mellin L$^2$ norm decays because
$1/\zeta(1/2+it)$ has controlled mean square---precisely the content of the
Hardy--Littlewood theorem.

This approach avoids the ``1D Shattering Trap'': real-variable bilinear
expansions require absolute values that destroy the delicate phase coherence
of the M\"obius sum, producing divergent bounds for a convergent quantity.

\section{Numerical Validation}

The \texttt{experiments/mellin-certificate/} Rust experiment (256-bit MPFR,
12 threads) independently validates the Parseval bridge and Mellin variance:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}rrrrl@{}}
\toprule
$N$ & Channel A & Channel B & $|A-B|/A$ & $C \cdot \log N$ \\
\midrule
100 & 0.13124 & 0.13119 & $3.8\times 10^{-4}$ & 0.604~$\checkmark$ \\
500 & 0.07313 & 0.07313 & $1.4\times 10^{-5}$ & 0.455~$\checkmark$ \\
1000 & 0.06032 & 0.06032 & $7.2\times 10^{-6}$ & 0.417~$\checkmark$ \\
2000 & 0.05012 & 0.05012 & $6.6\times 10^{-6}$ & 0.381~$\checkmark$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent Channel A computes $\int_0^1 (1-f_N)^2\,dx$ directly; Channel B
computes $\int_0^\infty |g_N(u)|^2\,du$ in log-space. Their agreement to
$6.6 \times 10^{-6}$ validates the Parseval bridge identity.
The constant $C \approx 0.38$ is still decreasing,
suggesting the true rate may be faster than $O(1/\log N)$.
