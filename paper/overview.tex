\begin{abstract}
We describe a Lean~4 formalization---the \emph{Cathedral}---that
constrains the 167-year-old Riemann Hypothesis through three independent,
machine-verified routes: a forward direction ($\mathrm{RH} \Rightarrow d^2 \to 0$),
a converse direction ($d^2 \to 0 \Rightarrow \mathrm{RH}$), and a discrete
arithmetic front via Robin's and Lagarias's inequalities.

A computer has verified 3,486 build steps across 45 Lean files with zero
errors and zero \texttt{sorry} placeholders. Thirty-six axioms remain,
each precisely identified and domain-isolated. If the axioms are proved---they
encode known results in number theory, real analysis, and spectral theory---the
Riemann Hypothesis follows.

As a standalone result, we prove \emph{unconditionally} and with zero axioms
that $\sigma(p) \leq H_p + e^{H_p}\ln H_p$ for all primes~$p$.
\end{abstract}

\section{What Did We Do?}

The Riemann Hypothesis (RH) is one of mathematics' deepest unsolved problems.
It concerns the distribution of prime numbers and has resisted proof since 1859.

We used a computer proof assistant called \textbf{Lean~4} to check every
logical step of three independent proof routes, each constraining RH from
a different direction. The computer verified 3,486 build jobs across 45 files
with zero errors and zero gaps.

\textbf{We did not prove RH.} We built a \emph{machine-verified framework}
that identifies exactly which mathematical facts remain to be formalized,
proves everything else with compiler-checked rigor, and connects three
independent routes into a unified architecture.

\section{The Three Routes to RH}

\subsection{Route 1: The Forward Direction (2 axioms)}

If the Riemann Hypothesis is true, then a certain $L^2$ distance $d_N^2$
converges to zero. The proof requires two axioms:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Mertens Bound} (Number Theory): RH implies that the
  sum of the M\"obius function $M(x) = \sum_{n\leq x}\mu(n)$ satisfies
  $|M(x)| \leq C\sqrt{x}(\log x)^2$.

\item \textbf{The Abel Summation Bound} (Real Analysis): Given the Mertens
  bound, summation by parts yields weights with $L^2$ error $\leq C/\log N$.
\end{enumerate}

A number theorist can prove Axiom~1 without knowing what a Gram matrix is.
A real analyst can prove Axiom~2 without knowing what a prime number is.
The axioms are completely \emph{domain-isolated}.

\subsection{Route 2: The Converse Direction (4 axioms)}

If the $L^2$ distance $d_N^2$ converges to zero, then the Riemann Hypothesis
must be true. The proof uses the \textbf{B\'aez-Duarte Orthogonal Witness}:
a zero $\rho$ of $\zeta$ off the critical line creates a rigid lower bound
$d_N^2 \geq |1/\rho|^2/\|h_\rho\|^2 > 0$ that cannot be spoofed.

Three axioms encode properties of the witness function $h_\rho$; a fourth
encodes the separation obstruction at zeros of $\zeta$.

\subsection{Route 3: The Robin/Lagarias Front (2 axioms + proved results)}

Two classical equivalences connect RH to elementary divisor-sum inequalities:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Robin's inequality:} $\sigma(n) < e^\gamma n \ln\ln n$ for
  $n \geq 5041$ iff RH.
\item \textbf{Lagarias's inequality:} $\sigma(n) \leq H_n + e^{H_n}\ln H_n$
  for all $n$ iff RH.
\end{itemize}

These are encoded as two axioms. Additionally, we proved \emph{unconditionally}:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Theorem} (\texttt{lagarias\_for\_primes}):
$\sigma(p) \leq H_p + e^{H_p}\ln H_p$ for ALL primes $p$.\\
\emph{Zero sorry. Zero axioms. Kernel-verified.}
\end{quote}

\section{The Architectural Bypasses}

The key innovation is that we \emph{re-engineered the mathematics}
to fit the frontier of current formalization technology, rather than
waiting for libraries to build up 20th-century complex analysis.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Autocorrelation Bypass:}
  Mathlib~4 lacks the $L^2(\mathbb{R})$ Plancherel isometry.
  We bypassed this by converting Mellin transforms to Fourier transforms
  via the substitution $x = e^{-u}$, defining the autocorrelation
  $h = g \star \tilde{g}$, and evaluating $h(0)$ via \emph{$L^1$ Fourier
  inversion}---a much weaker (and more tractable) theorem.

\item \textbf{The Mertens/Tauberian Bypass:}
  Standard proofs require Perron's formula and contour integration in
  $\mathbb{C}$. We excised the complex plane entirely, using the
  real-variable equivalence $\text{RH} \Leftrightarrow M(x) = \mathcal{O}(x^{1/2+\varepsilon})$
  and constructing optimal weights via Abel summation.

\item \textbf{The Pole Neutralization:}
  Naive $L^2$ approximants cause the norm to explode due to a $1/x$ pole
  (the ``Hyperplane Trap''). We defeated this algebraically by shifting
  a single weight ($v_2$) to enforce $\sum k v_k = 0$, completely
  neutralizing the pole. This was proved as a \emph{theorem} (zero sorry).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Three Discoveries}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The Sawtooth Floor:} A bound that seemed obviously true
informally turned out to be \emph{false} for large values.
The computer found a subtle constant-covariance effect ($C_\infty \approx 0.00227$)
that destroys the naive pointwise approach.

\item \textbf{The Hyperplane Trap:} Simple Cauchy--Schwarz with a linear
functional fails because ``spoofing'' weights exist that make the
functional vanish while the $L^2$ norm explodes. The fix required the
B\'aez-Duarte \emph{orthogonal} witness.

\item \textbf{The Prime Bucket Mechanism:} When a 128-bit MPFR optimizer
was given the Gram matrix with no knowledge of primes, it independently
discovered the M\"obius function $\mu(k)$, assigning negative weights to
primes and positive weights to semiprimes. This is the parity barrier
of Selberg, manifested numerically.
\end{enumerate}
